I started Vanish Proxies in 2020 to supply the demand of fresh IPs for the sneaker
reselling industry. There were three offerings provided, all with different usages.

## What we sold

### Residential proxies

Residential proxies are generally what you would consider ‘household devices’.
Generally, a person may download an app and not read its terms of service and may
end up giving permission to use their device as a network proxy. These types of IPs
are very useful in looking like a human, and are sold by the GB. Vanish resold a
very large proxy provider and would filter IPs and test them to give clean IPs by
site catering.

### Datacenter proxies

Datacenter proxies were never designed to look like humans, but rather to be the
fastest offering. It is common knowledge that companies that host ecommerce require
datacenters to provide fast and close-to-customer locations. Given that our largest
demand was for Shopify, we rented a colocation in Ashburn, VA at CenturyLink
(Lumen) and rented subnets to sell to our customers. Because they were in the same
location as Shopify servers we had 1ms ping for our customers. However, Shopify as
well as the individual administrators behind these ecommerce shops would often ban
subnets depending on the traffic, so checking the subnet and preparing backups was
a common practice.

### Private subnets

Some of Vanish’s clients were operating reselling and botting businesses in large
volume that we made deals for private subnets, and would usually take a multiple
month commitment to host full subnets for them.
