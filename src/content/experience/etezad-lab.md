Dr. Etezad has dedicated her research to designing and researching how to make
braille displays cheaper and more accessible to those who need it. My favorite
contribution while working there was the design of the “Pixel Art Braille
Display”.

A standard display looks as follows.

![One braille cell on a small backplate: two columns of three pins, four of them raised.](/img/braille-cell.svg)

In common design, braille displays are made in 2 × 3, which allows for a single
character to be displayed. Coming from a background of enjoying pixel art, I
noticed that these individual displays can be chained to make an 8 × 9 grid of
dots. By removing the bottom row, we have an 8 × 8 grid perfect for rendering
pixel art and limited shapes. In theory, this iconography can help a user
visualize what is on the computer in a media format as well as a text format.

This new design allows 3 words per row with proper spacing of a single line.

![Two braille backplates side by side, each eight dots across and nine dots down: the left one with no pins raised, the right one with pins raised in its top and bottom rows of cells](/img/braille-grid-hi.svg)

Additionally with the 8 × 9 grid we can draw pixel art.

![Four eight by nine braille grids, each drawing one icon in raised pins: a heart, a plus, a minus and an X.](/img/braille-icons.svg)

The work was presented at the university symposium and shown at conferences.
